How do I opt contacts out with a HubSpot workflow (GDPR)?

Short answer

Mostly you do not, and that is the point. Consent lives in subscription types, and the one-click unsubscribe already suppresses future sends for that type without any workflow. What a workflow adds is the rest: one unified suppression list, a cascade that unenrolls the contact from nurtures already running, a legal basis captured at the form, and the flip to non-marketing that stops you paying for someone nobody may email. Marketing Hub Professional, around thirty minutes per spec.

What breaks, and how the specs prevent it

Consent lives in subscription types, not in a property

A custom Consent given checkbox feels like the right place to store it, and it controls nothing. Sends are suppressed per subscription type, so a contact who unsubscribed from one type still receives every other type they never opted out of. If unsubscribe is meant to mean everything, every marketing email needs a type your all-types branch actually covers. And the direction back is not symmetrical: the Manage communication subscriptions action cannot resubscribe a contact who opted out themselves, so clearing a property never undoes an opt-out.

Marketing contact status is a bill, not a consent record

Setting a contact to non-marketing removes them from marketing sends and ads audience sync. It does not delete the record, it does not stop internal automation such as tasks and deal workflows, and it is not a substitute for suppression on a legal basis. It is a billing flag. It is a useful one, which is why six specs here work on it, but treating it as a consent decision gets both halves wrong. Reactivation counts against the tier immediately, so a bulk reactivation can create an overage the same afternoon.

An opt-out does not stop a workflow already running

Adding a contact to a global suppression list prevents future enrollment into workflows configured to respect that list. It does not pull someone out of a nurture they are already mid-flight in. There is no native mechanism that extends the cascade for you either, so each nurture has to be named in an explicit unenroll step and that list has to be updated every time a new nurture launches. A nurture that checks subscription status only at enrollment will still deliver one more email.

What this bundle builds

Two questions that look unrelated and share one answer. The first is asked by whoever holds the compliance file: can we prove why we are allowed to email this person, and can we stop within a day when they ask us to. The second is asked by whoever reads the invoice: why are we paying for forty thousand marketing contacts when half of them have opted out of everything.

Twenty three specs handle both. At the form, a submission routes by region into the right consent path, the legal basis gets written to the native property rather than a homemade one, and imports land in a backfill queue instead of an assumption. Through the life of the contact, per-type subscriptions are read as the real state, every nurture is gated on them, and a withdrawal cascades into a suppression list plus explicit unenrolments from the sequences already running. Before a basis goes stale, a legitimate interest review and a re-permission campaign fire on a date rather than on someone’s memory. On the billing side, contacts nobody may email flip to non-marketing, reactivations pass an approval gate, and two alerts warn before the tier is breached.

Nothing here is legal advice. It is what the automation layer can and cannot enforce, written down.

How the pieces fit together

The legal basis property first, because most of what follows branches on it and because it is a settings question, not a workflow one. With data privacy settings on, the native property is available and a mirrored custom property is only needed if you deliberately keep those settings off.

Then the capture path: region segmentation on the form, the source based routing, and the double opt-in confirmation for the cases that need one. Build the import backfill queue in the same pass, since that is where the awkward records live.

The subscription layer third. The eligibility gate is the piece everything else leans on, so decide early whether it is one reusable workflow or a block copied into each nurture, and write that down. The withdrawal cascade comes after it, since the cascade names the nurtures it unenrolls from and those need to exist first.

Billing last, and wired together on purpose. The all-types opt-out spec flips contacts to non-marketing, and the approval gate is what lets one of them come back when they opt into a single type again. Build only one of the two and you will quietly suppress a willing subscriber.

Questions admins ask

How do I opt a contact out of marketing email with a HubSpot workflow?

The suppression itself is not the workflow's job. The one-click unsubscribe already stops future sends for that subscription type. The workflow around it does four things the unsubscribe does not: it records a unified opt-out status you can filter on, adds the contact to a shared suppression list, unenrolls them from each named nurture they are currently inside, and notifies whoever needs to know. Around twenty five minutes on Marketing Hub Professional.

What is the difference between a marketing contact and a subscription opt-out?

They are two separate systems that people constantly merge. Marketing contact status decides whether a contact counts against your paid tier and whether any marketing send can reach them at all. A subscription opt-out is the contact's own preference about one channel or one type of email. A marketing contact can be opted out of the type you are about to send. A non-marketing contact receives nothing regardless of what they are subscribed to. Any eligibility gate has to check both.

Can a workflow delete a contact for a right to erasure request?

No. A workflow cannot perform the deletion, so the spec here builds the queue instead: log the request the moment it arrives, set a status, create a task with a deadline reminder, and escalate if the deadline approaches untouched. The deletion is done by a person with the right permissions, through the interface or the API. The value of the workflow is that no request is lost between an inbox and a spreadsheet.

Can a workflow resubscribe someone who unsubscribed?

Not one who opted out themselves. HubSpot blocks that path in the Manage communication subscriptions action, and in most European frameworks resubscription is expected to come from a contact-initiated action anyway. What the bundle does cover is the case where a contact opts back in through your preference centre or a form: a short welcome-back sequence, so they do not drop straight into whatever send was already queued. This is not legal advice, and the boundary is worth confirming with your own counsel.

Does setting contacts to non-marketing reduce the bill straight away?

It reduces the count your tier is measured against, so it changes the next billing cycle rather than the invoice you already have. Two specs give you the numbers before the decision: one alerts when the active marketing contact count approaches the plan limit, and one projects the count at the next billing date from the trailing growth trend and estimates the overage in currency. Both need Data Hub Professional, because the count is a custom coded query.
